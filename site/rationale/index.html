01

The previous site could belong to anyone.

The previous leavory.com was built from the defaults of AI-generated SaaS pages. Each piece worked on its own; together they read as a template, what people now call AI slop. And blue is the default colour of security software, so next to the rest of the category the site simply disappeared.

The signs of a template

  • A blue gradient hero. The colour, and the composition, of most of the category.
  • A shield icon in a rounded square. It reads as an app icon, not as a mark.
  • Icon tiles on identical cards. The same card, four times per section, in every section.
  • Blue arrow links everywhere. Every card points somewhere else, so nothing stands out.
  • Glowing blue buttons and a theme toggle. Features of the template, not of the product.
  • No idea of its own. Take the name off and it could sell any security product.

02

Personality, held together by a system.

The brief was not a new coat of paint. It was a site that could only be Leavory’s, and that stays that way as it grows.

  1. 01

    Personality

    A look that is recognisably Leavory’s, built from the product’s own story instead of the category’s habits.

  2. 02

    Consistency

    One colour logic, one type pairing, one line language, used the same way in every section.

  3. 03

    One system

    Everything documented in a design system generated from the page itself, so every new page starts from the same parts.

03

The decisions.

Four choices carry the identity. Each one comes from what Leavory does.

Red, black and white. Nothing else.

In security, red already means act on this. Leavory’s whole job is telling a team what to fix first, so red is reserved for exactly that: critical findings, the Fix step, the mark. The background is a warm ink, not the navy of the category, and white carries the reading.

Every other tone on the page is one of these four with transparency. An automated test fails if any other colour reaches the screen.

  • Red#C62A3D
  • Red type#D21E38
  • Ink#1E1C1B
  • White#FFFFFF

A reading face and a data face.

Geist sets everything you read, tight and confident in the headlines. IBM Plex Mono sets labels, numbers and data: the repository names, severities and steps that are the product’s own language. Large headlines split into two tones, grey then white, so the point lands on the second half.

Lines instead of boxes.

Structure comes from hairlines and rules, not from cards. Fields of vertical lines frame the hero and the call to action. Glass is kept for what floats or responds, like the header and the controls. Icon tiles gave way to custom isometric figures, lit from above, with one red piece each.

Motion that explains.

Every animation tells part of the story: results travel from the pipelines, a sweep finds the vulnerabilities, the ones that matter leave for the list. A button in the hero pauses everything, and with reduced motion turned on the page shows a still frame instead.

04

Drawn, not photographed.

The previous site had almost no imagery: text, icons and cards. Leavory needed pictures, but what it sells is abstract: pipelines, advisories, findings. Stock photos of padlocks and hooded hackers would have been the generic answer. The answer here is illustration and motion, drawn from the product itself.

  1. 01

    Ours alone

    Every figure is drawn for Leavory, in one system: tonal shapes lit from the top left, no outlines, one red piece for what matters. No stock library can put it on a competitor’s site.

  2. 02

    The abstract, made visible

    Each one shows an idea a photo cannot: an organization’s layers with one repository flagged, a finding explained, new advisories arriving.

  3. 03

    Motion that means something

    Movement is small and specific: the flagged repository bobs, the newest advisory slides in, data travels along the hero’s wires. It shows the product at work, not decoration.

  4. 04

    Light and lasting

    Everything is vector and code: sharp on any screen, lighter than photography, and nothing to reshoot when the product changes. With reduced motion, it all holds still.

05

References, made our own.

Two sites pointed the way for the hero and the motion. Nothing was copied: the layout, the code and the content are original.

Hero component

torq.io

What we took
A dark field with particles in depth and tonal tiles floating in it: a living system on the first screen instead of a screenshot of the product.
What we made ours
The particles form a sphere, which is Leavory. The tiles are the customer’s pipelines and the public sources, wired into it. The four steps run live until the findings that matter leave, red, for the Fix first list.

Lines and motion

serro.ai

What we took
Lines as the structure of the page, and motion that runs along them.
What we made ours
Vertical hairlines every 12px with short bars drifting along them, one red per field. The same line comes back in the striped wordmark of the footer and in the cuts of the mark.

06

The hero is the product, in four steps.

Instead of a picture of a dashboard, the first screen shows what Leavory does, live, in the time it takes to read the headline.

  1. 01

    Your pipelines and public sources

    Azure DevOps, GitHub, GitLab and Trivy on top; OSV, NVD, KEV and EPSS below. Each group enters through its own port.

  2. 02

    Leavory

    A sphere of particles, linked by a network of data. The findings hide inside it until the Cross-reference sweep finds them, and a target locks onto each one.

  3. 03

    The four steps

    Connect, Scan, Cross-reference, Fix, with live status notes. Fix is the only step that turns red.

  4. 04

    Fix first

    The sphere mirrors the list: the two Critical findings shine the brightest, then Secret and High, while minor ones only blink.

07

A shield that lets results through.

The previous mark was a shield icon in a blue tile. The new one keeps the shield, so it still reads as security at a glance, and cuts it open to say what Leavory does differently.

  1. The shield

    The most familiar sign in security, kept on purpose. A new visitor knows the category before reading a word.

  2. The cuts

    Leavory never reads your source code: only results, the SBOM and scan artifacts leave the pipeline. The mark draws that promise. It is not a closed wall but a shield with a way through. The name carries the same idea: leave.

  3. The diagonal

    Rising lines give the mark a direction, from finding to fix. They are the same line that structures the whole site, from the hairlines to the striped wordmark, so the mark and the system share one gesture.

  4. Three

    The fewest lines that make a rhythm, and the three platforms Leavory connects: Azure DevOps, GitHub and GitLab.

  5. The red

    The mark is the one red that is always on screen. Everything else red on the page is something to act on.

09

Five rules that keep it Leavory.

The identity holds as long as these do. Every new page, and every change to this one, can be checked against them.

  1. 01

    Red means act

    Red marks what needs action, the Fix step and the mark. It is never decoration.

  2. 02

    Four colours

    Red, red type, ink and white, with transparency. Anything else is off-brand, and a test says so.

  3. 03

    Lines before boxes

    Hairlines and rules build the structure. Glass is only for what floats or responds.

  4. 04

    Motion explains

    Every animation tells part of the product’s story, and every one can be paused.

  5. 05

    Data speaks mono

    Names, numbers, steps and labels in IBM Plex Mono; everything you read in Geist.
